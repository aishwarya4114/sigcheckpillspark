The problem

Pharmacies still retype blurry faxes by hand.

Slow

Every line of every fax is decoded and retyped by a person.

Ambiguous

"5.0 mg" can be read as 50 mg. Hydroxyzine looks like hydralazine. "Take as directed" says nothing.

Risky to automate badly

A confident AI that guesses turns one typo into a wrong drug or dose, silently.

What SigCheck does

It answers one question: what's the next safe action?

Fax arrives→Read + safety screen→Checks→Next action→Pharmacist resolves→Attested approval→Draft + audit
Not all uncertainty is the same

Three kinds of issue, three different fixes.

Verify transcription

The software isn't sure what the fax says. Check it against the highlighted image, confirm or correct.

Clarify with the prescriber

The fax itself is ambiguous or unsafe. Re-reading can't fix "5.0 mg". Only an unambiguous answer from the prescriber resolves it.

Blocked document

Text that tries to instruct the software stops the document before any model reads it.

Trust

The model reads. It never decides.

  • Verbatim extraction; anything unsure stays empty, never guessed
  • Deterministic checks the AI can't influence: NPI / DEA check digits, trailing zeros, look-alike drugs, catalogue coverage
  • Every field shows where on the fax it came from
  • Safety rules are locked on; approval needs a pharmacist's attestation

Under the hood

Sponsors behind the workflow, not in the pharmacist's way.

Nicrron

Structured extraction through the gateway (Claude Sonnet 5), verbatim with nulls for anything unsure.

PRISM by Block Convey

Guardrail traces, an evaluator on the classification, and the knowledge base grounding catalogue and policy claims.

Privacy

OCR on the device; PRISM gets statuses and codes with identifiers masked. Tested: no identifier reaches PRISM.
